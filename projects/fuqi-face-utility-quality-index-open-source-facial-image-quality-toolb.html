PR25. FUQI: Face Utility Quality Index — Open-Source Facial Image Quality Toolbox Based on ISO/IEC 29794-5 (CITeR 25F-04W)

  • Dates: Fall 2026 · Role: co-wrote the funded proposal; lead researcher; first author of the final report and presenter at the Fall 2026 CITeR Program Review · Team: Aaron Storey (Clarkson), Dr. Prashnna Gyawali (West Virginia University), Dr. Masudul Imtiaz (Clarkson)
  • Funding: NSF CITeR project 25F-04W ($45,000; multi-university, Clarkson and WVU); co-wrote the proposal
  • Status: final report presented at the Fall 2026 CITeR Program Review; open-source GitHub release (OFIQ + FUQI toolbox), written executive summary (Spring 2027), and a paper on partner-free utility labels and interpretable face quality planned
  • Overview: Face recognition fails most often on poor photos (blur, pose, lighting, occlusion, low resolution), and operators need to know before enrolment or search whether a photo is good enough and what to fix. Existing tools give either many separate measurements or a single unexplained score. FUQI (Face Utility Quality Index) is one score that predicts whether a face photo will be recognized, learned from real face-recognition results and built on the ISO/IEC 29794-5 (OFIQ) quality components, so it also points to the cause. It is the face counterpart of the Voice Quality Index (PR1).
  • Duties:
    • Framed the problem as utility-based face image quality (ISO/IEC 29794-1 "quality as utility") on top of the ISO/IEC 29794-5 components, carrying the VQI approach from voice to face
    • Set up OFIQ v1.0.3 (the open-source ISO/IEC 29794-5 reference implementation) and CR-FIQA, and selected and downloaded 7 public face datasets: CelebA, XQLFW, CPLFW, CALFW, AgeDB, TinyFace, and CFP (web photos, degraded copies, large pose and age gaps, wide age range, native low-resolution faces, frontal and profile photos)
    • Scored 432,549 images with all 28 OFIQ measures (native and 0–100 scales: lighting and exposure, sharpness, compression, colour, background, head pose, face size and framing, eyes, mouth and expression, occlusion, and the unified quality score UQS) and with CR-FIQA, and handled failures such as tiny faces where face detection fails
    • Built a person-disjoint 80/20 train / test split with 5-fold cross-validation, kept people who appear in several datasets together, held CFP out completely as an unseen dataset, and kept CFP people out of training
    • Aligned and embedded 279,121 faces with 4 label matchers (AdaFace IR-101 on WebFace12M, ArcFace ResNet-100 on Glint360K, LVFace-B transformer on Glint360K, EdgeFace-Base) and kept FaceNet (Inception-ResNet on VGGFace2) as an independent, held-out test matcher; verified each matcher (AUC, EER, TAR at FMR 1e-3)
    • Designed continuous consensus utility labels: cosine similarity of embeddings, each photo's "stranger level" (mean similarity to reference strangers), same-person scores corrected by the two photos' stranger levels, near-duplicates removed, a least-squares split of every same-person score into each photo's own share (removing the partner photo's effect), per-matcher percentiles, and the mean over the 4 matchers
    • Found and fixed two label problems: the first label followed the quality of a person's other photos (fixed with each photo's own share), and S-norm normalisation tracked quality for LVFace (fixed with mean-only normalisation)
    • Trained two FUQI variants, FUQI-C (27 OFIQ components only, fully explainable) and FUQI-CU (components plus UQS), with 4 tree models (LightGBM, XGBoost, Random Forest, CatBoost) and 3 dataset weightings under 5-fold participant-disjoint cross-validation
    • Evaluated with ISO/IEC 29794-1 Error-versus-Discard Characteristic (EDC / ERC) curves and pAUC-based gain on 624k same-person and 14M different-person comparisons at FMR 1e-3 across 5 matchers, DET curves by quality thirds, Spearman rank agreement, quality-bin staircases, and per-component analysis
    • Checked OFIQ components against CelebA attribute labels (hats, glasses, smiles, open mouths, blur) with Cliff's delta and one-sided Mann-Whitney tests, finding that OFIQ Sharpness misses blurry photos while UQS and CR-FIQA catch them
    • Compared FUQI with the two leading scores (OFIQ UQS and CR-FIQA), reported honestly where CR-FIQA stays ahead, and analyzed why its advantage does not carry over to an unseen matcher (training-data overlap with the label matchers)
    • Kept the large pipeline reliable with checkpoint checks that caught OFIQ text-encoding and non-ASCII path issues, detector crashes on tiny faces, and GPU library mismatches; ran embedding extraction in batches on GPUs and stored per-image tables for analysis
    • Used LLM coding assistants (Claude Code, GitHub Copilot) to implement and debug the scoring, labelling, and evaluation code, reviewing every change
    • Wrote the final report and presented it to CITeR industry and government members at the Fall 2026 Program Review; prepared the deliverables (FUQI models, Python scoring and evaluation code using OFIQ v1.0.3, derived per-image quality tables and utility labels for the 7 datasets without redistributing images, evaluation workbook)
  • Tech stack: Python, OFIQ v1.0.3 (C++ reference implementation of ISO/IEC 29794-5), CR-FIQA, AdaFace, ArcFace, LVFace, EdgeFace, FaceNet, InsightFace-style face alignment, PyTorch, ONNX Runtime, OpenCV, NumPy, pandas, SciPy, scikit-learn, LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost, Matplotlib; NVIDIA GPUs; LLM coding assistants (Claude Code, GitHub Copilot)
  • Highlights:
    • Co-wrote the funded CITeR proposal ($45K) and led the project that built FUQI, an open, explainable face image quality score that predicts whether a photo will be recognized, on top of the ISO/IEC 29794-5 (OFIQ) quality components.
    • Built consensus utility labels from several deep face matchers (AdaFace, ArcFace, LVFace, EdgeFace), with a held-out matcher (FaceNet) and a held-out dataset for honest testing.
    • Trained explainable tree models (LightGBM, XGBoost, Random Forest, CatBoost) and evaluated them with ISO/IEC 29794-1 error-versus-discard and DET analysis against OFIQ and CR-FIQA.
    • Presented the final report to CITeR industry and government members at the Fall 2026 Program Review.
  • Outputs: CITeR final report and presentation (T7); open-source toolbox and executive summary (Spring 2027); paper planned
