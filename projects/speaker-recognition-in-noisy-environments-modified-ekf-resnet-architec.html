PR14. Speaker Recognition in Noisy Environments: Modified EKF-ResNet Architecture (CITeR 23S-07C)

  • Dates: January 2024 – Fall 2024 (progress reported at the CITeR Spring 2024 Program Review; invited talk at the International Martigny Biometrics Workshop, May 2025)
  • Role: lead student researcher; co-wrote the funded proposal
  • Team: A. Ahmed, S. Purnapatra, Dr. M. Imtiaz (Clarkson University)
  • Funding: NSF CITeR (IUCRC), $45K (see Research Funding)
  • Overview: Noisy environments sharply reduce automatic speaker recognition accuracy, and most public datasets simulate noise artificially. This project built a voice preprocessing and denoising pipeline, assembled real-world noisy and longitudinal voice datasets, and measured how denoising affects commercial speaker verification (Neurotechnology VeriSpeak), comparing classical (Kalman filter) and deep-learning (Wave-U-Net) denoising. Findings fed directly into the VPQAD dataset, the speech-enhancement benchmark (BDCC 2026), and the Voice Quality Index.
  • Duties:
    • Reviewed the literature on speaker recognition under noise, speech enhancement, and speech-quality metrics
    • Designed and ran real-world noisy adult voice collection (51 subjects in cafeterias and teaching labs with live background speech), including the IRB protocol, recording setup, and participant scheduling
    • Curated evaluation data: the Clarkson longitudinal child voice dataset (14+ collections over 7+ years, ages 4–18) and the public SpEAR corpus (clean speech with controlled Gaussian noise)
    • Built the preprocessing pipeline: resampling, normalization, high-pass Butterworth filtering, (extended) Kalman filtering, energy-based VAD segmentation, framing (1.5–46.5 ms, 50% overlap), MFCC extraction
    • Implemented and adapted Wave-U-Net (1D CNN source separation trained on MUSDB18 / ccMixter) for speech denoising
    • Built evaluation tooling: frame-wise FFT SNR, before / after quality metrics, and VeriSpeak 1:1, 1:N, and N:N match scoring across subjects, collections, and ages
    • Trained and tested a neural-network speaker-recognition baseline on SpEAR (train / validation / test splits)
    • Analyzed why the Kalman filter was inconsistent across subjects and moved to deep-learning denoising; proposed a ResNet speaker model and a meta-learning (MAML) denoiser for future work
    • Wrote progress reports and presented to CITeR industry and government members; managed code, data, and documentation
  • Key results:
    • Kalman filtering raised SNR substantially on some samples (e.g. from 0.23 dB to 37.65 dB) but was inconsistent across subjects
    • Wave-U-Net increased child-voice SNR by 26.99–53.88 dB and consistently improved VeriSpeak match scores across collections
    • Speaker-recognition accuracy on the SpEAR test set: 30% unfiltered → 60% with the Kalman filter → 70% with Wave-U-Net
  • Tech stack:
    • Languages: Python, MATLAB
    • Signal processing: SciPy signal, NumPy (FFT), librosa, FilterPy / pykalman, MATLAB Signal Processing Toolbox (butter, filtfilt), webrtcvad / energy VAD, python_speech_features
    • Deep learning: PyTorch / TensorFlow (Wave-U-Net), ResNet speaker embeddings; MUSDB18, ccMixter training data
    • Biometrics: Neurotechnology VeriSpeak SDK (1:1, 1:N, N:N matching)
    • Data and tools: Audacity, Audio-Technica AT2020 and Shure SM58 microphones, Jupyter, Git / GitHub, Matplotlib
  • Highlights:
    • Led a CITeR-funded ($45K) project on speaker recognition in noisy environments, building a denoising and preprocessing pipeline (Kalman filter, VAD, MFCC, Wave-U-Net) evaluated with a commercial speaker-recognition engine (Neurotechnology VeriSpeak).
    • Collected a real-world noisy voice dataset from 51 adults in cafeterias and labs, and combined it with a 7-year longitudinal child voice corpus (ages 4–18) and the SpEAR benchmark for validation.
    • Raised speaker-recognition accuracy from 30% to 70% on noisy test data and increased child-voice SNR by up to 54 dB using deep-learning (Wave-U-Net) denoising, outperforming classical Kalman filtering.
    • Presented results to industry and government sponsors (CITeR Program Review) and in an invited talk at the International Martigny Biometrics Workshop (Switzerland); the findings led to the VPQAD dataset and the Voice Quality Index.
