Honors & Awards

  • 1st Prize, 3-Minute Thesis (3MT) Competition, Research and Project Showcase (RAPS), Clarkson University, Spring 2024 (April 2024). Won the 3MT competition with a 3-minute, single-slide talk for a non-specialist audience on the same research as the Best Graduate Oral Presentation: how speech emotionality affects speaker recognition accuracy.
  • Best Graduate Oral Presentation, Computer Science and Computational Modeling category, Research and Project Showcase (RAPS), Clarkson University, April 19, 2024.
    • Presentation: "The Influence of Speech Emotionality on ASR Metrics and Recognition Accuracy" (single author). Examined how emotion changes pitch, tone, speed, and volume and so degrades automatic speaker recognition; proposed a three-part approach (emotion-diverse annotated data collection, preprocessing with MFCC / pitch / tone / speed features, and CNN / LSTM models trained with cross-entropy loss). Reported lower FAR and FRR across 8 emotions (e.g. anger FRR 0.20 to 0.17, happiness FRR 0.05 to 0.02), and outlined future work on transformers and multi-task learning of speaker and emotion.
    • Highlights: Won Best Graduate Oral Presentation at Clarkson's Research and Project Showcase for research on how speech emotion affects speaker recognition, showing lower false-acceptance and false-rejection rates across 8 emotional states with CNN / LSTM models.
  • Best Presenter Award (Poster), Mathematics Conference and Competition of Northern New York (MCCNNY), April 2024.
    • Poster: A. Ahmed, M. Imtiaz, and S. Schuckers, "Quantifying the Impact of Deep Learning-Enhanced Speech Preprocessing on ASR Accuracy." Applied CNN-based denoising to noisy speech (MS-SNSD dataset), measured speech quality before and after denoising (SNR, MOS, PESQ), measured speaker recognition performance with Neurotechnology VeriSpeak, and correlated the quality metrics with recognition accuracy to find which metrics predict it; this line of work led to the Signals paper and VQI.
    • Highlights: Won Best Presenter (Poster) at MCCNNY 2024 for research correlating deep-learning speech enhancement and speech-quality metrics (SNR, MOS, PESQ) with commercial speaker-recognition accuracy.
  • Merit Scholarship ($6,750; 50% tuition), Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Clarkson University, Spring 2024.
