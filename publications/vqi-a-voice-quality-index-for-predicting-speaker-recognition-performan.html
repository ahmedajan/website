J1. VQI: A Voice Quality Index for Predicting Speaker Recognition Performance

  • Reference: A. Ahmed, S. Schuckers, and M. H. Imtiaz, "VQI: A voice quality index for predicting speaker recognition performance," IEEE Access, vol. 14, pp. 94103–94121, 2026, doi: 10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3703023.
  • Status: Published June 2026 · first and corresponding author · code: github.com/ahmedajan/VQI-v1 · funding: CITeR 2413228, NSF 2501916
  • Abstract: Speaker recognition systems are increasingly deployed in security, forensics, and authentication, yet no standardized metric exists to predict the quality of a voice sample for recognition purposes. Existing audio quality metrics such as the Perceptual Evaluation of Speech Quality (PESQ) and Perceptual Objective Listening Quality Prediction (POLQA) measure perceptual quality rather than recognition utility, defined as a sample's ability to produce accurate recognition outcomes. We propose the Voice Quality Index (VQI), the first utility-based voice quality metric designed to predict speaker verification performance. VQI employs a dual-score architecture producing two complementary integer scores on a 0–100 scale: VQI-S for signal quality and VQI-V for voice distinctiveness. We extract 544 signal-quality and 161 voice-distinctiveness candidate features from each audio sample, selecting a subset of features through a three-stage pipeline. Random Forest classifiers trained on 20,288 balanced samples drawn from 1.2 million utterances across eight datasets produce utility-based quality scores. Evaluation using Error vs. Reject Curves on three test datasets (VoxCeleb1, VCTK, CN-Celeb) with five speaker verification providers demonstrates that rejecting the lowest-quality 20% of comparisons reduces the false non-match rate (FNMR) by up to 38.0% at the 1% FNMR threshold. A Principal Component Analysis (PCA)-based dimensionality reduction pipeline (77% for signal quality, 65% for voice distinctiveness) enables efficient deployment with minimal accuracy loss. VQI generalizes across English datasets and partially across languages, with quality-ordered partitions consistently separating system performance. Per-file processing latency ranges from 1.9 to 36.7 seconds, positioning VQI as an offline and batch quality-assessment tool rather than a real-time enrollment filter. An open-source desktop application is provided for practical deployment.
  • Highlights:
    • Invented the Voice Quality Index (VQI), the first utility-based voice quality metric that predicts speaker verification performance, aligned with ISO/IEC 29794-1; published as first and corresponding author in IEEE Access.
    • Engineered 705 explainable acoustic features (544 signal-quality, 161 voice-distinctiveness) and a 3-stage feature-selection pipeline (variance, Spearman correlation, Random Forest importance).
    • Built a consensus labelling pipeline over 1.2 M utterances from 8 datasets using 3 speaker verification systems (ECAPA-TDNN, ResNet34-SE, ECAPA2) with s-norm scoring, yielding a balanced 20,288-sample training set.
    • Cut false non-match rate by up to 38.0% by rejecting the lowest-quality 20% of comparisons, validated with Error-vs-Reject Curves on 3 test sets and 5 verification systems, including 2 never seen in training.
    • Reduced feature dimensionality by 77% (signal) and 65% (voice) with PCA at minimal accuracy loss, enabling efficient deployment.
    • Shipped an open-source desktop app and CLI (github.com/ahmedajan/VQI-v1) with dual 0–100 scores and per-feature explanations; led peer review and revisions as corresponding author.
